# English phrasing by function

Choose expressions after identifying what the sentence needs to do. The examples below are original and illustrate typical functions of academic English. Replace their contexts with the author's verified content, and keep terminology, tense and spelling appropriate to the field and venue.

| Function | Writing decision | Original example |
|---|---|---|
| Qualify certainty or scope | Name the claim being qualified and the source of uncertainty. Distinguish uncertainty about an estimate from uncertainty about applicability. | *In the sites sampled, the difference was concentrated in the final hour.* / *The estimate was sensitive to the choice of comparison sites.* |
| Critique a study or approach | Identify the specific methodological feature, its consequence for interpretation and a feasible remedy when useful. Credit what the study established. | *The audit captures demand while the library is open, leaving unmet demand outside those hours unmeasured.* |
| Classify or list | State the dimension and category criteria. A parallel list helps the reader see how categories relate. | *We classified sites by their weekday closing schedule: before 18:00, 18:00 to 20:00, or after 20:00.* |
| Define a term | Give its broader category and distinguishing feature. Specify the operational definition where measurements depend on it. | *Here, repeat use refers to at least two recorded visits by the same patron within a calendar month.* |
| Compare or contrast | Keep the metric, unit, time and population aligned. Explicitly name the comparator and the basis for any numerical difference. | *Both groups used the same definition of a visit. The municipal records cover a wider range of sites.* |
| Describe a relationship or cause | Use association language for a relationship. Match causal verbs to identification and mechanism evidence. | *Later closing was associated with more visits per weekday.* / *The evening peak may reflect differences in commuters' schedules.* |
| Give an example | State the general point first when the example illustrates it. Explain how a case bears on a claim when it is evidence. | *Access barriers differed by time of day. At one site, interviews linked the evening barrier to a gap in bus service.* |
| Signal a transition | Identify the relationship that advances the argument. Repeat a key term when it creates a clearer link than a generic connective. | *These site-level averages obscure when the difference arose. The hourly analysis identifies the period of greatest divergence.* |

## Calibrate wording to evidence

| Basis for the sentence | Expression choice |
|---|---|
| Direct observation or adequately supported estimate | Report what was measured or observed with a scoped subject and a concrete verb, such as *recorded*, *observed*, *estimated* or *was higher*. |
| Pattern compatible with more than one explanation | Use *is consistent with*, *suggests* or *may reflect* for the supported inference. Identify a competing explanation if it matters. |
| Proposed mechanism | State the proposed link and what kind of evidence would distinguish it. Use *could arise from* or *one possible explanation is* where appropriate. |
| Causal inference supported by the design | Name the intervention and contrast, check the assumptions and choose a causal verb whose scope matches the outcome, comparison and setting. Evidence of an effect alone may leave the pathway uncertain. |
| Imprecise estimate or absent test | Report the estimate and uncertainty, or say what was not assessed. A non-significant test by itself cannot establish equivalence or absence of an effect. |

Qualify the exact point of uncertainty. For example, *The difference was observed at one site* qualifies scope, while *Site characteristics may explain the difference* qualifies the explanation. A cautious adverb cannot turn an unsupported causal claim into a supported one. Avoid piling qualifiers onto a sentence whose meaning is clearer with a narrower subject.

## Adapt phrases to the manuscript

- Use the past for completed procedures and specific observations when that fits the venue. Use the present for a figure's content or an interpretation stated in the paper. The present perfect can connect a body of prior work to the current question. Keep shifts purposeful.
- Choose the subject for emphasis. *We measured* makes the actor clear. *Samples were measured* can be useful when the procedure or material is the focus. For citations, verbs such as *reported*, *estimated* and *proposed* identify different kinds of contribution.
- For Chinese-to-English work, recover the implied actor, comparator and reference of each pronoun. Use a concrete verb in place of vague formulas, preserve quantifiers, and define an acronym or term as the venue requires. Verify terms for measured and modeled quantities.
- When adapting common academic language, use generic expressions and your own subject matter. Attribute source-specific ideas, interpretations, data and quotations to the relevant source. Follow the venue's rules for quotations and acknowledgment.
