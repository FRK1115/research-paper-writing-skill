# Revision and source checks

## Revise in the order that exposes substantive problems

1. **Argument.** Write the research question and the paper's answer side by side. Trace the answer to results and the results to methods. Trace background and novelty claims to verified literature. Bring under-supported claims to the author's attention with the smallest usable correction or evidence request.
2. **Structure.** Reverse-outline a draft. Check whether paragraphs answer the question their opening raises and whether later sections depend on observations already established. Align the abstract, headings and conclusion with the main contribution. Move lengthy supporting material to appendices or supplements if the venue permits and readers can still judge the central inference.
3. **Sentence and document checks.** Verify terminology, abbreviations, time periods, populations, comparators, units, sample sizes and figures against the source material. Check table and figure callouts, denominator changes, citations, and unexplained causal or novelty language. Edit grammar and concision after those checks.
4. **Venue requirements.** For submission tasks, consult current author instructions for article type, limits, permitted section format, citation style, declarations, acknowledgments and relevant disclosure requirements. Use target-journal priorities only when applicable to the manuscript. For course work, follow the assignment and instructor's rules. Attribute use of AI where the applicable rules require it.

## Handle evidence and references

- For each substantive claim drawn from external work, locate the source and check that its study, passage or analysis supports the particular population, comparator, relationship or interpretation being cited. Use primary evidence for a specific finding and high-quality synthesis for claims about the state of the field where appropriate. Cite commentary for the commentator's interpretation.
- Paraphrase from understanding, preserve attribution, and check that quotations are exact and properly marked. Reconcile in-text citations with the bibliography. Record enough bibliographic detail to retrieve the source and distinguish it from similarly titled work.
- Lead with journal, Journal Impact Factor and year when reporting journal literature to this user. For the Chinese Academy of Sciences journal partition, identify the edition, major discipline and subcategory when relevant and verified. Distinguish CAS partitions from JCR quartiles. Record unknown, unverified or inapplicable metrics explicitly. When metrics are relevant, prefer the applicable JCR or official publisher information and the relevant CAS edition. These metrics do not establish whether a given source supports a claim.
- For cross-study statements, track agreement, definition differences, contradictory evidence and strength of inference. Calibrate novelty claims to the scope and date of the search. Bracket uncertain references or fact gaps rather than creating placeholder author names, journal details or DOIs.

## Worked example using fictional material

**Input.** A cross-sectional survey in one city asked about commute mode and weekday library visits during the previous month. Public-transit commuters reported more frequent visits than car commuters. The survey did not measure proximity to transit stops or track transit access over time.

| Section | What the evidence permits |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Ask whether weekday library use differs by commute mode in this surveyed population. Establish the gap with verified literature if the author wishes to claim it. |
| Methods | Describe survey recruitment, the month and population sampled, definitions of commute mode and visit frequency, and the analysis actually performed. |
| Results | *Respondents who commuted by public transit reported more frequent weekday library visits than respondents who drove.* Add the estimate, its uncertainty and denominators when supplied. |
| Discussion | *The difference may reflect proximity to transit stops, although the survey did not measure residential proximity.* Leave competing explanations open. |
| Conclusion | *In this city, commute mode was associated with self-reported weekday library use. Comparing visit patterns before and after a transit service change could help assess how access affects use.* |

For a proposal using the same topic, keep the question and planned measurements and withhold the observed-result sentences until evidence is available. For a request to polish one sentence, deliver the supported association wording and explain any change of claim strength briefly.
